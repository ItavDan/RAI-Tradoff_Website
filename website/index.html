Cancer Risk Assessment & Insurance Coverage

Faced with rising healthcare costs and the need for efficient resource allocation, a major insurance company is deploying an Automated Decision System (ADS) using machine learning. The goal is to identify policyholders who are at a high risk of developing severe cancer. The screening system focuses on medical records and insurance claims.

Based on the patient's initial claims and additional information pulled from medical and public databases, the system will automatically decide which cases to write off as standard risk, and which cases to flag as requiring comprehensive (and expensive) oncology screening and premium adjustments.

Ground Truth

In our historical data, 30% of the patients are at high risk a. Patients at high risk are those who will develop severe cancer within two years. The other 70% are not at high risk b.

Model Predictions

In a perfect world, only patients truly at risk would be flagged as requiring comprehensive oncology screening, and only patients who are actually healthy would be flagged as not requiring additional screening.

Model Mistakes

But models (and also humans) aren't perfect.

The model might make a mistake and miss a patient who is actually at high risk of cancer c.

Or the opposite: the model might flag a patient as high risk f, while in fact they are perfectly healthy and no additional screening or premium hike is required.

Never Miss a Cancer Diagnosis...

One approach would be to have the model flag patients “aggressively”, such that close calls are always marked as “high risk”, ensuring the model rarely misses a potential cancer patient.

... Or Avoid Unnecessary Screenings & Costs?

On the other hand, expensive medical resources would be allocated to healthy individuals, resulting in wasted funds, unjustified insurance premium hikes, and severe unnecessary anxiety for the patients.

These issues and trade-offs in model optimization aren't new, but they're brought into focus when we can fine-tune exactly how aggressively the model works when flagging patients for cancer risk.
